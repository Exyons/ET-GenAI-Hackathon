# Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani) - ICAR Advisory

## Symptoms
Early blight of tomato, caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*, is characterized by brown to black spots with concentric rings on the lower, older leaves. The spots often have a yellow halo. As the disease progresses, leaves may turn entirely yellow and drop off. Dark, sunken lesions may also appear on the stems and fruit.

## Environmental Triggers
High humidity (above 80%), heavy dew, and moderate to warm temperatures (24-29°C) strongly favor the development of early blight.

## Recommended Treatments
1. **Cultural Practices:** Ensure proper plant spacing for air circulation. Avoid overhead irrigation. Remove and destroy infected plant debris. Practice crop rotation (avoid planting tomatoes or related crops like potatoes in the same field for at least 2 years).
2. **Chemical Control (Approved):** 
   - Preventative: Spray Mancozeb 75% WP at 2.0 g/litre of water.
   - Curative: Spray Chlorothalonil 75% WP at 2.0 g/litre of water, or Azoxystrobin 23.6% SC at 1.0 ml/litre.
   - Repeat sprays every 7-10 days depending on weather conditions.
3. **Biological Control:** Spray *Trichoderma viride* or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* at 5-10 g/litre.

**WARNING:** Do not harvest tomatoes for at least 5 days after applying Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil to ensure pesticide residue safety.
