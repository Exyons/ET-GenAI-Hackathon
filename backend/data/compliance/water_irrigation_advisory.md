# Water & Irrigation Management - ICAR Advisory

**Source:** ICAR-Indian Institute of Water Management (IIWM), Bhubaneswar; ICAR-Central Institute of Post-Harvest Engineering & Technology (CIPHET)
**Applicable To:** All crops, All regions

## Irrigation Methods

### Flood/Surface Irrigation (Traditional)
- Most common in India (>85% irrigated area)
- Efficiency: 30-40% (60-70% of water is WASTED)
- Suitable for rice (paddy needs standing water)
- **Improvement:** Laser land leveling increases efficiency to 50-60%; saves 20-25% water

### Drip Irrigation
- Most efficient: 90-95% water use efficiency
- Water savings: 40-60% compared to flood irrigation
- Yield increase: 20-100% depending on crop
- **Best for:** Vegetables, fruits, sugarcane, cotton, flowers
- **Components:** Main line, sub-main, laterals, drippers (emitters)
- **Dripper spacing:** Based on crop spacing and soil type
- **Subsidy:** 55% for small/marginal farmers under PMKSY

### Sprinkler Irrigation
- Efficiency: 70-80%
- Water savings: 30-40% compared to flood
- **Best for:** Wheat, pulses, oilseeds, groundnut, fodder crops
- **Types:** Portable sprinklers (most common), center pivot, rain gun
- **Not suitable for:** Rice, heavy clay soils, windy areas
- **Subsidy:** 55% for small/marginal farmers

### Micro-sprinkler
- Efficiency: 80-85%
- Good for closely spaced crops and nurseries
- Lower pressure requirement than sprinklers

## When to Irrigate — Decision Tools

### IW/CPE Ratio Method
- Irrigate when cumulative pan evaporation (CPE) equals a set value
- Example: If IW/CPE = 0.8 and IW depth = 5 cm → irrigate when CPE accumulates to 6.25 cm
- Measure CPE using USWB Class A pan evaporimeter (available at most KVK/research stations)

### Tensiometer (Soil Moisture Tension)
- Install at 15-30 cm depth in root zone
- Irrigate when reading reaches crop-specific threshold
- Rice: irrigate at 15-20 kPa; Wheat: 40-50 kPa; Vegetables: 30-40 kPa
- Low-cost, farmer-friendly tool — contact KVK for guidance

### Visual Signs of Water Stress
- Leaf rolling/wilting during afternoon
- Dark green/bluish colour in rice
- Footprints remain visible when walking through rice field (no water)
- Cracking of soil surface

## Water-Saving Technologies

### System of Rice Intensification (SRI)
- **Principle:** Younger seedlings (8-12 days), single seedling/hill, wider spacing (25x25 cm), alternate wetting-drying (AWD)
- **Water saving:** 30-40% compared to continuous flooding
- **Yield increase:** 20-30% with proper management
- **AWD:** Flood to 5 cm → let water recede till hairline cracks appear → re-flood. Use field water tube (perforated pipe) to monitor
- **Not suitable when:** Red rot is a risk (needs flooding) or extreme drought

### Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)
- Skip nursery and transplanting — directly sow pre-germinated seed in puddled field or dry seed in dry field
- **Water saving:** 15-20% (skip puddling and nursery water)
- **Labour saving:** 30-40% (no nursery, no transplanting)
- **Herbicide:** Pendimethalin pre-emergence is essential (weed pressure is higher in DSR)
- **Seed rate:** 25-30 kg/hectare (higher than transplanting)

### Raised Bed Planting (wheat)
- Plant wheat on raised beds (10-15 cm high) with furrows for irrigation
- Water saving: 30-40%
- Uses bed planter machine
- Furrow irrigation eliminates flooding
- Additional benefits: less lodging, easier intercultural operations

### Mulching
- **Plastic mulch:** Black/silver polyethylene; best for vegetables; conserves moisture, controls weeds, increases soil temperature
- **Organic mulch:** Crop residue, straw, dried leaves; 5-10 cm thick; moderates soil temperature, adds organic matter
- Water saving: 25-35%

## Rainwater Harvesting

### Farm Pond
- Dig farm pond (10x10x3m or larger) to collect monsoon runoff
- Line with HDPE/LDPE sheet (250 micron) to prevent seepage
- Use stored water for supplemental irrigation during dry spells
- **Subsidy available** under PMKSY and MGNREGA

### Check Dams and Percolation Tanks
- Community-level structures in hilly/undulating terrain
- Recharge groundwater; provide surface water for irrigation
- Constructed under MGNREGA or watershed development programs

### Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting
- Collect roof water into underground tank or farm pond
- 1 mm rainfall on 100 sq.m roof = 100 litres water
- Average 800mm rainfall → 80,000 litres/year from 100 sq.m roof

## Groundwater Management
- **Problem:** India uses 25% of global groundwater — largest user in the world
- **Overexploited blocks:** Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu — water table dropping 1-3 m/year
- **Solutions:**
  - Shift from rice-wheat to less water-intensive crops (millets, pulses)
  - Recharge through farm ponds, percolation pits, recharge wells
  - Drip/sprinkler adoption (reduces pumping by 40-60%)
  - Laser leveling reduces runoff and improves infiltration
  - Community regulation of pumping hours

## Water Quality for Irrigation
| Parameter | Good | Moderate | Poor |
|-----------|------|----------|------|
| EC (dS/m) | <0.75 | 0.75-2.25 | >2.25 |
| SAR | <10 | 10-18 | >18 |
| RSC (me/l) | <1.25 | 1.25-2.5 | >2.5 |
| pH | 6.5-8.0 | 8.0-8.5 | >8.5 |

- **High SAR water:** Causes sodic soils; apply gypsum
- **High EC water:** Causes salinity; use drip irrigation; leach salts
- **High RSC water:** Neutralize with gypsum before use
- Get water tested at KVK or state agriculture university lab

## Fertigation (Fertilizer through Drip)
- Apply water-soluble fertilizers through drip system
- Efficiency: 80-90% nutrient use efficiency (vs 30-50% for conventional)
- **Suitable fertilizers:** Urea, MAP (12-61-0), SOP (0-0-50), Calcium Nitrate, Potassium Nitrate
- **Schedule:** Small doses at frequent intervals (daily or alternate day)
- **Saving:** 25-30% fertilizer reduction with same or higher yield
- **Crops:** All drip-irrigated crops — vegetables, fruits, sugarcane, cotton
