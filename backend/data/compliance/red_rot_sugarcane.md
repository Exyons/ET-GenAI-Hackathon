# Red Rot in Sugarcane (Colletotrichum falcatum) - ICAR Advisory

## Symptoms
Red rot, caused by *Colletotrichum falcatum*, is one of the most devastating diseases of sugarcane. Early symptoms include the yellowing and drooping of the 3rd or 4th leaf from the top. Later, the entire crown dries up. The most characteristic symptom is seen when the cane is split lengthwise: the internal tissue is reddened with distinct white horizontal patches interrupting the red areas. The infected canes emit a sour, alcoholic smell.

## Environmental Triggers
High soil moisture, waterlogging, and high humidity during the monsoon season favor the spread. The disease is primarily seed-borne (transmitted through infected setts) but can also spread through soil and irrigation water.

## Recommended Treatments
1. **Cultural Practices (Primary Defense):**
   - **Crucial:** Once a clump is infected, there is NO curative chemical treatment that can save it. The infected clump must be completely uprooted and burned.
   - Crop rotation with paddy or green manure crops for 2-3 years.
   - Improve field drainage to avoid waterlogging.
   - Use healthy, disease-free seed setts from a recognized nursery.
2. **Preventative Seed Treatment:** 
   - Sett treatment with Carbendazim 50% WP at 0.1% (1g/litre) for 15 minutes before planting.
   - Heat therapy (Moist Hot Air Treatment) of setts at 54°C for 2.5 hours.
3. **Varietal Selection:** Plant highly resistant or moderately resistant varieties recommended for your specific agro-climatic zone. Avoid planting highly susceptible varieties like Co 0238 in endemic areas.
