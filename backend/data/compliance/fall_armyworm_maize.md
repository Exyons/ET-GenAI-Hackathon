# Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) in Maize - ICAR Advisory

## Symptoms
The Fall Armyworm (FAW) is a highly destructive pest primarily affecting maize (corn). Early symptoms include elongated papery windows on the leaves caused by first and second instar larvae. Later stages involve extensive defoliation, with ragged holes in the leaves. The larvae can also bore into the whorl (central shoot), causing a "dead heart" symptom, and may attack the developing cobs.

## Environmental Triggers
FAW can persist year-round in warm climates and spreads rapidly. Dry weather followed by sudden rain can trigger outbreaks.

## Recommended Treatments
1. **Cultural Practices:** Deep summer ploughing to expose pupae to sun and predators. Early and synchronized planting. Intercropping maize with legumes (like pigeon pea or cowpea) can reduce infestation. Hand-picking and destroying egg masses and young larvae if feasible.
2. **Biological Control:** 
   - Apply *Metarhizium rileyi* (formerly *Nomuraea rileyi*) at 5g/litre.
   - Release egg parasitoids like *Trichogramma pretiosum*.
3. **Chemical Control (Approved):** 
   - First Window (Seedling to Early Whorl): Spray Emamectin benzoate 5% SG at 0.4 g/litre or Spinetoram 11.7% SC at 0.5 ml/litre.
   - Second Window (Late Whorl): If damage exceeds 10%, apply granular insecticides like whorl application of Carbofuran 3G (use with caution).
   - **Important:** Direct the spray nozzle towards the whorl of the plant for maximum effectiveness.
