# Soil Health Management - ICAR Advisory

**Source:** ICAR-Indian Institute of Soil Science (IISS), Bhopal; Soil Health Card Scheme, Government of India
**Applicable To:** All crops, All regions

## Soil Health Card (SHC) Scheme
- Every farmer is entitled to a free Soil Health Card from the government
- SHC is issued once every 2 years with NPK + micronutrient status
- Contact your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or state agriculture department for testing
- Collect soil samples properly: zigzag pattern, 15 cm depth, 8-10 spots per field, mix and send 500g
- **Do NOT sample:** near bunds, trees, manure heaps, compost pits, or waterlogged areas

## Soil pH Management

### Acidic Soils (pH < 6.0)
- Common in: Northeast India, Kerala, Western Ghats, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh
- **Treatment:** Apply agricultural lime (CaCO3) at 2-4 tonnes/hectare based on soil test
- Apply lime 3-4 weeks before sowing; mix thoroughly in top 15 cm
- Dolomite limestone preferred if Mg is also deficient
- Acidic soils fix phosphorus — apply higher P doses or use rock phosphate

### Alkaline/Sodic Soils (pH > 8.5)
- Common in: Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan, Gujarat (Indo-Gangetic plains)
- **Treatment:** Apply Gypsum (CaSO4) at 50% of Gypsum Requirement (GR) as determined by soil test
- Typical dose: 5-10 tonnes/hectare for reclamation
- Ensure adequate drainage and leaching irrigation after gypsum application
- Grow tolerant crops first: Rice > Wheat > Berseem > then gradually other crops
- Add organic matter (FYM, green manure) to improve soil structure

### Saline Soils (EC > 4 dS/m)
- **Treatment:** Install subsurface drainage; leach salts with good quality water
- Grow salt-tolerant varieties: CSR 36, CSR 46 (rice), KRL 210 (wheat)
- Apply FYM liberally (10 tonnes/hectare) to improve water retention and reduce salt stress

## Organic Matter and Carbon
- Indian soils are critically low in organic carbon (most < 0.5%)
- **Target:** Maintain soil organic carbon above 0.75%
- **How to increase:**
  1. Apply well-decomposed FYM/compost at 10-15 tonnes/hectare/year
  2. Green manuring: Grow and incorporate Dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata) or Sunhemp (Crotalaria juncea) — provides 60-80 kg N/hectare equivalent
  3. Crop residue retention (do NOT burn stubble — it destroys organic matter and beneficial microbes)
  4. Cover cropping during fallow periods
  5. Vermicompost at 5 tonnes/hectare — concentrated organic matter + beneficial microbes

## Micronutrient Deficiencies

### Zinc Deficiency (most widespread in India)
- **Symptoms:** Interveinal chlorosis in young leaves; stunted growth; delayed maturity
- **Crops most affected:** Rice, wheat, maize, soybean
- **Treatment:** Soil application of ZnSO4 at 25 kg/hectare as basal; foliar spray of ZnSO4 0.5% at flowering
- **50% of Indian soils are zinc deficient** — regular application is essential

### Iron Deficiency (Lime-induced chlorosis)
- **Symptoms:** Young leaves turn completely yellow/white while veins remain green initially
- **Common in:** Alkaline calcareous soils (pH > 8.0)
- **Crops affected:** Rice (nursery), groundnut, soybean, sorghum
- **Treatment:** Foliar spray of FeSO4 1% + Citric acid 0.1% at 10-day intervals (3-4 sprays)
- Soil application of FeSO4 at 50 kg/hectare is less effective (gets fixed in alkaline soils)

### Boron Deficiency
- **Symptoms:** Hollow stem in cauliflower/broccoli; poor fruit set; cracked fruits in tomato; hollow heart in potato
- **Crops affected:** Cauliflower, sunflower, cotton, groundnut, apple
- **Treatment:** Soil application of Borax at 10 kg/hectare OR Foliar spray of Borax 0.2% at flowering
- **Warning:** Boron toxicity range is narrow — do not exceed recommended dose

### Manganese Deficiency
- **Symptoms:** Interveinal chlorosis in young leaves (similar to iron); grey speck in oats
- **Common in:** Sandy soils, high pH soils
- **Treatment:** Foliar spray of MnSO4 0.5% at 2-3 week intervals

### Sulphur Deficiency
- **Symptoms:** Uniform yellowing of young leaves (unlike nitrogen which affects old leaves first)
- **Crops affected:** Oilseeds (mustard, soybean, groundnut), pulses, onion, garlic
- **Treatment:** Apply elemental sulphur at 40 kg/hectare or Gypsum at 200-250 kg/hectare as basal
- Sulphur is essential for oil content in oilseeds and pungency in onion/garlic

## Bio-fertilizers (ICAR Recommended)

### Nitrogen Fixers
- **Rhizobium:** For legumes (soybean, chickpea, pigeon pea, groundnut). Strain-specific — use correct strain for each crop
- **Azotobacter:** For non-legumes (wheat, rice, cotton, vegetables). Fixes 20-40 kg N/hectare/year
- **Azospirillum:** For cereals and millets. Apply as seed treatment or root dip
- **Azolla:** For rice. Dual crop or incorporate. Fixes 30-40 kg N/hectare/season
- **Blue Green Algae (BGA):** For flooded rice. Apply 10 kg dried BGA flakes/hectare in standing water

### Phosphate Solubilizers
- **PSB (Bacillus megaterium, Pseudomonas striata):** Solubilize fixed soil phosphorus. Apply at 5g/kg seed
- Makes 15-25% more P available from applied fertilizers

### Potash Mobilizers
- **Frateuria aurantia:** Mobilizes potash from soil minerals. Apply at 5g/kg seed

### Mycorrhiza (VAM)
- Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi extend root zone by 10-100x
- Apply 10 kg VAM inoculum/hectare in nursery or at planting
- Benefits: Enhanced P uptake, drought tolerance, disease resistance
- Avoid excessive P fertilization (>40 kg P2O5) — suppresses mycorrhizal colonization

## Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) Principle
1. Get soil tested every 2 years via Soil Health Card
2. Apply fertilizers based on soil test (not blanket recommendation)
3. Combine 75% chemical fertilizer + 25% organic sources (FYM/vermicompost + biofertilizers)
4. Apply micronutrients based on deficiency symptoms or soil test
5. Include legume in crop rotation (fixes 50-200 kg N/hectare depending on crop)
6. Green manuring once every 2-3 years
7. Never burn crop residues — incorporate or mulch
